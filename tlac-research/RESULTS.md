# TLAC instruments, US investors and bail-in: findings and HSBC ownership pilot

Research completed on 21 September 2026. The ownership pilot measures holdings on **30 June 2025**. Other findings carry their own dates and geographic coverage.

A **G-SIB** is a bank designated as globally systemically important. **Total loss-absorbing capacity (TLAC)** is the capital and eligible liabilities available to absorb losses and help restore a failing bank. It includes ordinary equity as well as certain other securities. A **bail-in** imposes losses on investors, including by reducing debt or converting it into shares. **MREL** is a related European and UK requirement with a broader bank population; an MREL statistic is not automatically a global G-SIB statistic.

The strongest new finding is that a defined group of US registered investment funds reported about **$8.799 billion of principal within a selected inventory of 129 HSBC Holdings plc instruments**, equivalent to **5.43% of the inventory's combined $161.957 billion principal**, on 30 June 2025. This is an observed share for a particular issuer and investor population, not total US ownership of HSBC or of global TLAC. The detailed qualifications below are part of that finding.

## 1. What percentage of G-SIB TLAC instruments are owned by US investors?

**We do not yet have a defensible global percentage.** Two published results establish US participation but cannot be used as the answer to this question:

- A Bank of Italy study reports an average **9.4% US share of the holdings visible in its dataset** over 2014 Q1–2020 Q2. It covers TLAC bonds of eight euro-area G-SIBs. Its observation of investors outside the euro area depends on their use of reporting custodians, so missing holdings can change the result. Source: Carmela Aurora Attinà and Pierluigi Bologna, [*TLAC-eligible debt: who holds it? A view from the euro area*, pp. 12–13](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/qef/2021-0604/QEF_604_21.pdf).
- The ESM reports a **12% US share of investment funds' holdings of its sampled euro-area bank MREL bonds** at year-end 2023. This has a different bank population, investor population and denominator. Details appear under question 2.

These are not two estimates of the same quantity. They do not justify a “9–12%” global ownership range.

For the user's primary interest, economic exposure, the owner of a fund and the fund itself must also be distinguished. A US resident can hold a Luxembourg fund that owns a bank bond. A foreign resident can hold a US fund. Fund location alone cannot allocate the ultimate gains and losses by investor residence.

**What would close the gap practically:** select a common date and assemble (1) the G-SIB list and legal issuing entities; (2) every eligible external security's identifier, outstanding principal, currency and eligibility dates; (3) security-level holdings with holder residence and holder type; and (4) fund-share ownership by investor residence if ultimate exposure is required. Add prices for a market-value measure and hedging positions for a net-exposure measure. Internal financing between companies in the same banking group must be excluded from an external-investor total.

The FSB's [2025 G-SIB list](https://www.fsb.org/2025/11/2025-list-of-global-systemically-important-banks-g-sibs/) contains 29 banking groups and is a practical starting population for a year-end 2025 study. The missing input is primarily comprehensive ownership, not a list of banks.

## 2. What percentage of G-SIB TLAC instruments are owned by US investment funds or other types of US organization?

**For investment funds, there is published evidence and a reproducible public-data method. For insurers, pension funds, banks and direct household investors, this research has not established a percentage.**

The ESM reports approximately **€221 billion** of global investment-fund holdings of euro-area bank MREL bonds issued since 2017, measured at year-end 2023. The US accounts for **12%**, implying approximately **€26.52 billion**, calculated as €221 billion × 12%. Euro-area funds account for 69%. The analysis combines Lipper fund holdings with Dealogic bond data. These are market values and fund-location statistics, not the US share of the amount of bonds outstanding. Source: [ESM, *The hidden web: How loss-absorbing bonds connect banks and investment funds*, Figure 3](https://www.esm.europa.eu/blog/hidden-web-how-loss-absorbing-bonds-connect-banks-and-investment-funds).

**Useful public source:** SEC Form N-PORT provides identifiable fund positions, including principal, currency, market value and reporting date. It excludes money market funds from this reporting population. The [SEC bulk data](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets) and [data dictionary](https://www.sec.gov/files/nport_readme.pdf) enabled the HSBC calculation below. This is much stronger than treating a US asset manager's entire worldwide portfolio as US-owned.

**Practical next data by investor type:**

| Investor type | Data needed to calculate a percentage |
|---|---|
| US registered investment funds | The HSBC-style instrument match expanded to all G-SIBs, plus consistent holdings-date coverage and verification of legal domicile where residence matters |
| US insurance companies | Security identifiers, principal held and dates from insurer investment schedules, with insurer legal domicile and separate/general account identification; a practical route to investigate is NAIC Schedule D data or a licensed provider of it |
| US pension funds | Direct bond holdings from plan or custodian records, plus positions held through pooled funds; fund look-through must avoid counting both the fund and its investors |
| US banks | A security-level bank holdings dataset identifying the bank and instrument; broad balance-sheet categories do not identify which bonds qualify as TLAC |
| US households and private funds | Custodian or broker records identifying beneficial-owner residence and security quantities; private-fund exposures also need fund ownership information |

These are data requirements, not claims that those datasets have been obtained or that they cover every holder.

## 3. What percentage of HSBC's eligible instruments is held by US registered investment funds?

**Pilot answer: 5.43% of the principal in the selected HSBC Holdings plc inventory was reported by the observed funds on 30 June 2025.**

### Precisely what was measured

The issuer inventory comprises **129 distinct securities issued by HSBC Holdings plc**, taken from its June 2025 disclosure sections for Additional Tier 1 (AT1), Tier 2 counting toward MREL, and other eligible liabilities counting toward MREL. AT1 and Tier 2 are categories of bank capital securities; they sit below ordinary senior debt in the order investors are repaid. Ordinary shares and six instruments listed separately as Tier 2 that does not count toward MREL were excluded.

The denominator is the **full nominal principal of those securities**, translated to dollars using the dollar equivalents in HSBC's disclosure. It is not HSBC's entire consolidated TLAC, market capitalization, or the regulatory amount recognized after adjustments. It covers the named parent issuer, not a census of every HSBC subsidiary.

The numerator uses **US SEC-registered investment funds whose registrants report a US address**, with holdings dated exactly 30 June 2025. “US” here describes a transparent reporting cohort. The address field does not independently prove legal domicile, citizenship or residence of the fund's investors.

Sources: [HSBC instrument disclosure, pp. 4–14 and 16–43](https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/hsbc-results/2025/interim/pdfs/hsbc-holdings-plc/250806-capital-and-other-tlac-eligible-instruments-main-features-30-june-2025.pdf), matched to the SEC's [2025 Q2](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera/data/form-n-port-data-sets/2025q2_nport.zip), [Q3](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera/data/form-n-port-data-sets/2025q3_nport.zip) and [Q4](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera/data/form-n-port-data-sets/2025q4_nport.zip) N-PORT archives. The archive quarters describe filing coverage; the selected holdings date is June 30.

### Results by type

All dollar values below are billions. “Reported principal” uses the same exchange rate as the denominator for each security.

| Instrument category | Securities in inventory | Outstanding principal in inventory | Principal reported by observed funds | Observed share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AT1 | 14 | $20.942 | $0.571 | **2.73%** |
| Tier 2 counting toward MREL | 31 | $30.918 | $1.358 | **4.39%** |
| Other eligible liabilities counting toward MREL | 84 | $110.097 | $6.870 | **6.24%** |
| **Total** | **129** | **$161.957** | **$8.799** | **5.43%** |

The unrounded numerator is approximately **$8,798,951,701**. The calculation is $8,798,951,701 ÷ $161,957,000,000. The denominator's non-dollar equivalents are rounded in the issuer disclosure, so additional percentage decimal places would suggest excessive precision.

Additional concrete findings from the same calculation:

- **449 funds** contributed **1,883 included positions**. Matches were found for **91 of the 129 securities** before the unit exclusions described below. The absence of a match for the remainder means no match in the observed reports, not no US owner.
- The **66 US-dollar-denominated securities** had $106.152 billion of principal. Observed principal in them was $8.415 billion, or **7.93%**. This avoids currency-conversion assumptions. It does not imply that all dollar bonds are US-owned.
- The market value of the included positions was **$8.825 billion**. This is kept separate from the principal-based ownership fraction.
- Looking at all identified holdings before the unit exclusions gives **455 funds and $8.860 billion of reported market value**.

### Can we make a statement about one particular instrument or fund?

Yes. For HSBC security **US404280DC08**, the inventory reports **$2 billion principal**. Included funds report **$312.220 million**, giving an observed principal share of **15.61%**. Dodge & Cox Income Fund alone reports **$199.080 million principal**, or **9.954% of that security**, with a reported market value of approximately $194.914 million.

The Dodge & Cox holding was checked against the original filing, not just the bulk extraction. All **11 matched HSBC positions** for that fund agree between the two sources on principal and market value. Source: [Dodge & Cox Income Fund, June 30, 2025 N-PORT filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29440/000119312525191213/0001193125-25-191213-index.htm), accession 0001193125-25-191213. The HSBC denominator is on page 9 of the instrument disclosure.

### What this result leaves out

1. **Funds without an observable June 30 report.** The archives contained 6,571 selected US-address fund reports for that exact date after removing repeat reports. Reports for May or July were not substituted. This is not a census of all US funds' June 30 holdings, and no extrapolation was applied.
2. **Sixteen identified positions with ambiguous units.** They had $34.537 million of market value but reported the balance in units described as shares rather than principal. They were excluded from the principal numerator instead of assuming that one reported unit equals one dollar of principal. Their values remain in the evidence file.
3. **Other investor sectors and ultimate fund investors.** Insurance-company portfolios, direct pension holdings, households, private funds and many separate accounts are outside this calculation. The residence of people who own fund shares is not observed.
4. **Net exposure.** This is gross reported instrument exposure. Fifty-six included positions reported some securities on loan, with $61.882 million of loan value. These remain included because the fund reports the investment as its asset; they are not counted a second time. No included position was reported as securities merely received as non-cash collateral. Hedging and possible ownership chains are not consolidated.
5. **Later corrections.** The pilot uses archives through 2025 Q4; it does not incorporate amendments first filed in 2026.

Consequently, **5.43% is an observed share for this defined reporting cohort, not a statistical estimate of total US beneficial ownership or a guaranteed lower bound on ultimate US economic exposure**.

**What would close the practical gaps:** obtain missing funds' June 30 schedules, resolve the 16 unit anomalies against their original reports, check legal fund domicile, add other investor sectors, and extend the issuer inventory to other relevant HSBC entities. An identifier-alias table would also capture holdings reported under alternative trading identifiers for the same economic security. For a market-value ownership percentage, obtain June 30 prices for every security in the denominator. Do not divide the funds' market values by the issuer's regulatory TLAC total.

## 4. What is the total global value of TLAC instruments?

**No current global total was established in this research.** Three different quantities need separate answers:

| Intended total | Required calculation |
|---|---|
| Regulatory loss-absorbing resources, including ordinary equity | Sum consistent external TLAC amounts across G-SIBs or resolution groups, removing internal positions and duplicate group/subgroup reporting |
| Outstanding debt and other non-common-equity eligible securities | Sum each security's outstanding principal, with current eligibility and a common exchange-rate date |
| Market value of those securities | Apply contemporaneous prices to outstanding quantities; include common equity separately if the question includes it |

A sum of regulatory CET1 capital cannot be described as the market value of ordinary shares: regulatory capital includes retained earnings and adjustments. Likewise, accumulated issuance is not today's outstanding stock.

**What is needed:** a common-date table containing bank, legal entity, whether the figure is consolidated or for a resolution group, external versus internal amount, regulatory category, deductions, currency and reporting date. The HSBC disclosures demonstrate that these fields can be obtained for individual banks. The remaining practical work is collecting and reconciling them across the chosen G-SIB population; a global total has not been inferred from the single-bank pilot.

## 5. What is the total value of one G-SIB's TLAC instruments?

**HSBC provides two useful, different answers.**

- At **30 June 2025**, HSBC reported **$289.0 billion of consolidated regulatory TLAC after deductions**. This includes common equity and other qualifying resources. Source: [HSBC June 2025 Pillar 3 report, Table 15, p. 26](https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/hsbc-results/2025/interim/pdfs/hsbc-holdings-plc/250806-pillar-3-disclosures-at-30-june-2025.pdf).
- At the same date, the **129 parent-company securities selected for the pilot** had approximately **$161.957 billion of full nominal principal**, based on our instrument-by-instrument calculation. This excludes ordinary shares and is a different scope and measurement from consolidated regulatory TLAC.
- At **30 June 2026**, HSBC's consolidated regulatory TLAC after deductions was **$299.1 billion**. Source: [HSBC June 2026 Pillar 3 report, Table 15, p. 25](https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/hsbc-results/2026/interim/pdfs/hsbc-holdings-plc/260810-hsbc-holdings-plc-pillar-3-disclosures-at-30-june-2026.pdf).

The regulatory tables also show separate European, Asian and US resolution-group columns. Those columns should not simply be added to the consolidated total: the report explains adjustments for positions within the wider group.

**Remaining gap:** to turn the $161.957 billion instrument principal into a complete reconciliation with regulatory TLAC, we would need recognized amounts, adjustments, ordinary-equity components and relevant subsidiary instruments at the same date. The pilot does not claim that the difference between the two totals consists solely of common equity.

## 6. What is the value of G-SIB TLAC issuance in a particular year?

**The FSB provides direct historical annual estimates.** Its 2019 implementation review reports:

| Year | Estimated TLAC issuance |
|---|---:|
| 2016 | Approximately **$400 billion** |
| 2017 | Approximately **$433 billion** |
| 2018 | Approximately **$360 billion** |

The population is the G-SIBs covered by that review, rather than today's 29-bank list. The report's scope centers on banks subject to the January 2019 implementation date, with BPCE and RBC also participating; Chinese G-SIBs were outside that implementation population. The estimates use Bloomberg and FSB calculations. Source: [FSB, *Review of the Technical Implementation of the TLAC Standard*, July 2019, section 7.1, p. 31; scope on p. 1](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P020719.pdf).

**A recent related measure:** the SRB reports **€119.789 billion of gross MREL issuance in July–December 2025** for its reporting population of European resolution entities. Their MREL resources were **€2,658.672 billion at year-end**, increasing by only €17.479 billion over that half-year. This is broader than European G-SIBs and is not global TLAC issuance. Source: [SRB H2 2025 dashboard, Table 2, p. 13](https://www.srb.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-07-20_MREL-dashboard-H2-2025.pdf).

**A bank-level illustration:** UBS reports $3.302 billion of new TLAC-eligible senior unsecured debt during Q4 2025. Its recognized balance nevertheless fell from $104.379 billion to $96.105 billion after calls/eligibility removals, repurchases and other changes. Source: [UBS Q4 2025 report, p. 41](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1114446/000161052026000008/edgar4q25ubsgroup.htm).

**What remains missing:** a current global annual issuance total on a consistent definition. It requires issue and settlement dates, original amounts, currencies, increases to existing securities, eligibility at issuance and a chosen exchange-rate convention. Historical outstanding inventories alone omit securities subsequently redeemed. A decline in outstanding resources cannot be treated as negative gross issuance.

## 7. How many times have G-SIBs issued TLAC instruments in a particular year?

**There is a historical global count, and the HSBC pilot supplies a more recent but narrower count.**

The Bank of Italy paper identifies approximately **5,270 TLAC-eligible securities issued by G-SIBs globally during 2013–June 2020**. Eight euro-area G-SIBs account for **1,715 securities and €776 billion of issuance**. These are multi-year totals, not annual or currently outstanding counts. Source: [Attinà and Bologna, pp. 10–12](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/qef/2021-0604/QEF_604_21.pdf).

In the HSBC inventory, **18 of the 129 securities have an original issuance date in January–June 2025**:

| Category | Securities first issued in H1 2025 and present in the June inventory |
|---|---:|
| AT1 | 3 |
| Tier 2 counting toward MREL | 3 |
| Other eligible liabilities | 12 |
| **Total** | **18** |

Those securities were issued on **nine distinct dates** and had approximately **$24.016 billion of nominal principal at June 30 exchange rates**. That value is a surviving-inventory measure, not independently verified gross cash proceeds. Multiple securities can be issued in one financing transaction. A later increase to an existing security can retain its identifier and original issue date.

**What is missing:** a complete annual count of financing transactions or new security series. We would need final terms and issuance announcements, linked to issue identifiers, amounts and dates, with increases separated from first issues. The unit of counting must be specified: financing transactions, individual tranches, or identifiers. Separate identifiers used for placement formats must not count one economic security twice.

## 8. What is the breakdown by debt, convertible securities, equity and other instrument types?

**Both a regulatory breakdown and an instrument-principal breakdown can be measured, but they answer different questions.**

HSBC's **30 June 2026 consolidated regulatory TLAC** is:

| Component | Amount | Share of $299.1bn total, calculated |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common equity Tier 1 | $127.7bn | 42.7% |
| Eligible AT1 | $23.7bn | 7.9% |
| Eligible Tier 2 | $28.3bn | 9.5% |
| Non-regulatory-capital TLAC instruments | $119.4bn | 39.9% |
| **Total** | **$299.1bn** | **100.0%** |

Source: [HSBC June 2026 Pillar 3, Table 15](https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/hsbc-results/2026/interim/pdfs/hsbc-holdings-plc/260810-hsbc-holdings-plc-pillar-3-disclosures-at-30-june-2026.pdf). The June 2025 principal inventory and ownership breakdown is under question 3; its amounts should not be spliced into this June 2026 table.

For **2018 issuance**, the FSB's $360 billion estimate comprises **$36 billion AT1, $24 billion Tier 2, $52 billion non-preferred senior debt and $248 billion other senior debt**. These correspond to approximately **10.0%, 6.7%, 14.4% and 68.9%**, respectively. Source: [FSB 2019 implementation review, section 7.1, p. 31](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P020719.pdf).

The classification problem is real. HSBC's disclosure describes AT1 contingent convertible securities as shareholders' equity for accounting purposes. It also labels some ordinary senior bonds “convertible” because statutory bail-in powers can convert them. A single convertible/non-convertible field would therefore mix unlike mechanisms.

**What would produce the requested legal-form breakdown:** retain separate fields for regulatory category, accounting/legal form, and conversion or write-down mechanism. For every instrument record whether conversion is triggered by a contractual capital threshold or by a statutory resolution decision. Ordinary equity should have its own quantity and valuation method. This work requires security-level terms, not just an aggregate TLAC table.

## 9. How do contract details differ between instrument types, banks and countries?

**The reviewed documents demonstrate several materially different mechanisms. These are specific examples, not a claim that every instrument in a country uses the same terms.**

| Example | What triggers loss absorption or conversion? | What determines the investor's resulting claim? |
|---|---|---|
| HSBC UK AT1, US404280BL25 | Contractual trigger when the defined CET1 capital ratio falls below 7%; statutory powers are addressed separately | Contractual automatic-conversion terms, share-delivery procedures and investor notices |
| HSBC senior bond, XS1481811872 | Statutory UK bail-in powers | Conversion may be full or partial; rate determined at conversion, rather than a contractual capital-ratio conversion formula |
| RBC Canadian subordinated notes under the reviewed programme | A defined non-viability event and automatic contractual conversion | Shares calculated using 1.5 × note value ÷ conversion price; conversion price is the greater of the defined market price and a CAD5 floor, subject to adjustments |
| RBC Canadian senior bail-inable notes under that programme | A statutory Canadian bail-in conversion order | Senior investors must receive more common shares per dollar converted than junior instruments that are converted, as described in the prospectus |
| UBS Swiss bail-in debt covered by its 2026 SEC request | FINMA orders conversion after determining non-viability | Direct conversion into new UBS Group AG equity under the described resolution process |

**Sources and details that matter:**

- The HSBC AT1 security was issued as **$3 billion of 6% perpetual securities**, with a first call in May 2027. Its [supplemental indenture](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089113/000119312517177410/d401017dex41.htm), especially the trigger definition and sections 2.18–2.20, includes arrangements for a conversion-share depository, instructions through DTC and late investor notices. Its exhibits are templates, not evidence that a conversion occurred. [Issuance confirmation](https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/results-and-announcements/stock-exchange-announcements/2017/may/sea-170522-hsbc-contingent-convertible-securities-closing.pdf).
- HSBC's senior example is a **$2 billion, 4% bond due September 2036**, with mandatory cumulative payments and structural subordination. Source: [HSBC main-features disclosure, p. 17](https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/hsbc-results/2025/interim/pdfs/hsbc-holdings-plc/250806-capital-and-other-tlac-eligible-instruments-main-features-30-june-2025.pdf).
- RBC's programme distinguishes contractual non-viability conversion of subordinated notes from statutory bail-in of senior notes. Its subordinated conversion formula includes accrued unpaid interest and foreign-currency conversion, where applicable. Source: [RBC July 16, 2024 base prospectus, pp. 29–30 and 143–144](https://www.rbc.com/investor-relations/_assets-custom/pdf/european-senior-notes/UKLA-Notes-Base-Prospectus-July-16-2024.pdf). Individual final terms determine whether a particular issue belongs to the relevant category.
- UBS states that the debt covered by its request was issued under both **New York and Swiss law**, and was sold through registered, exempt and offshore offerings. Source: [UBS incoming SEC letter, July 8, 2026, pp. 2–4](https://www.sec.gov/files/corpfin/no-action/ubsgroupag-incoming-letter-070826.pdf).

**What is not yet measured:** the frequency of these terms across all G-SIB debt, differences across issuance vintages, or the proportion of US holdings exposed to each mechanism. To close that gap, extract issuer, identifier, governing law, creditor ranking, trigger, conversion price or authority discretion, treatment of unpaid interest, write-down permanence, call conditions and delivery procedure from a broader sample. Join those fields to the holdings dataset to turn a contract comparison into an exposure comparison.

## 10. What do we know about foreign G-SIB bail-ins and US securities law?

**There are directly relevant SEC staff positions from 2026. They make this a concrete legal and operational question rather than merely a hypothetical concern about US investors.**

### UK: an exchange through temporary rights can receive narrowly defined registration relief

On **10 April 2026**, SEC staff responded to the Bank of England. Staff stated that exchanging bail-in securities for ordinary shares, either directly or through interim securities, constitutes an offer and sale under the Securities Act. On the represented facts, staff would not recommend enforcement if the described two-stage exchange proceeded without registration in reliance on counsel's view that Section 3(a)(9) applies.

The described temporary rights, called PROPPs, go only to existing holders and are generally non-transferable. The issuer relationship is constrained: the same issuer, or the specified wholly owned subsidiary/full unconditional guarantee arrangement. This is a fact-specific staff enforcement position, not a blanket exemption or legal conclusion for every bail-in. Source: [SEC response to the Bank of England](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/no-action-interpretive-exemptive-letters/division-corporation-finance-no-action/bankofengland-04102026).

### Switzerland: UBS obtained a position for direct conversion into its own equity

On **8 July 2026**, SEC staff took a corresponding position for the described FINMA-directed conversion of UBS Group AG debt into new UBS Group AG equity. Staff again treated the exchange as an offer and sale and relied on the represented facts and counsel's Section 3(a)(9) analysis. The cited features include FINMA's non-viability determination, direct conversion and the same issuer for the debt and resulting equity. Source: [SEC response to UBS Group AG](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/no-action-interpretive-exemptive-letters/division-corporation-finance-no-action/ubsgroupag070826).

**Practical implication:** counting US holders is only one part of the problem. The legal issuer of the old and new securities, guarantees, the use of temporary rights, transferability and the precise exchange steps can affect the available registration route. These letters do not establish that an actual bail-in occurred or that all other US securities-law obligations disappear.

**Useful broader references:** the FSB's [2018 *Principles on Bail-in Execution*](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P210618-1.pdf), particularly Principles 11–14, organize the questions about securities-law compliance, disclosure, listing and issuance. Its [2021 work on practical bail-in execution](https://www.fsb.org/2021/12/fsb-illustrates-practical-approaches-to-the-execution-of-bail-in/) identifies cross-border problems involving trading suspensions, security depositories and delivery of new securities. These references are useful for comparing execution processes across jurisdictions, not for estimating ownership percentages.

**What remains to be checked for another bank:** the exact terms of the original security, the issuer of the replacement security, any guarantee, whether the holder receives temporary rights, whether a relevant SEC staff position covers the facts, and the status of the resulting security for registration, reporting, trading and resale. A foreign-law recognition clause alone does not answer these separate US-law questions.

## Pilot method, checks and files

The attached research is reproducible with [run_hsbc_pilot.py](run_hsbc_pilot.py). Copy [config.example.py](config.example.py) to `config.py`, set the data directory and a descriptive SEC User-Agent with a contact email, and run the script in Spyder. It uses the Python standard library plus the `pdftotext` command from Poppler. The three SEC archives total approximately 1.3 GB compressed; allow additional space for generated evidence. The script is intentionally fixed to the June 2025 disclosure and pilot date.

The principal-share calculation follows these steps:

1. Extract the issuer, identifier, original issue date, nominal amount and recognized regulatory amount from the 129 selected columns in the HSBC PDF. Check that all identifiers are distinct and pass the identifier check-digit test. Review the principal rows visually against the PDF.
2. Read submission, registrant and fund tables from the three SEC archives. Select only June 30, 2025 reports with US registrant addresses. For repeat reports of the same fund and date, retain the latest filing in those archives, using accession number to break same-day ties. Fund identity uses registrant plus series identifier, with documented fallbacks to a legal-entity identifier or name.
3. Match holdings using exact ISINs, which identify securities internationally, or the corresponding CUSIP for US-format identifiers. Do not rely on an issuer-name search for the final match. Reject conflicting identifier matches.
4. Include long holdings explicitly reported as principal, in the instrument's currency, classified as debt or preferred equity. Keep other identified positions in a separate exception file. This avoids silently interpreting shares as principal.
5. For each security, divide the reported native-currency principal by that security's native-currency nominal amount. For the aggregate, translate both numerator and denominator using that security's dollar equivalent in HSBC's disclosure. Unmatched securities stay in the denominator.
6. Check that no observed security share exceeds 100%, that holdings keys are unique, and that none of the included positions is securities received as non-cash collateral. Preserve the securities-lending flags. Independently compare original filing data to bulk values for the 11 Dodge & Cox matches and 13 BlackRock Strategic Income Opportunities Portfolio matches. All 24 agree on principal and market value. The latter check includes non-dollar positions; its accession is 0001752724-25-210414.

The final match scanned **16,540,783 holding rows across the three archives** and selected **1,883 included positions**. There were **6,596 US-address filings for the exact date before duplicate-report selection and 6,571 funds after it**. These counts describe the processed data, not the total population of all US investors.

Local supporting evidence is retained in `.cache/pilot/` in this folder. It is excluded from Git under the repository's data policy. The report and runner are the portable research record; the runner recreates the main evidence files from the linked public sources.

| Evidence file | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `pilot-summary.json` | Totals, category calculations and coverage counts |
| `pilot-instruments.json` | All 129 denominators, amounts, source pages and observed shares |
| `pilot-holdings.json` | Included fund positions with reporting dates, identifiers and filing links |
| `pilot-excluded.json` | The 16 positions excluded from the principal calculation |
| `pilot-selected-filings.json` | The exact-date fund-report population after duplicate selection |
| `pilot-lending.json` | Collateral and loan flags for the included positions |
| `pilot-source-manifest.json` | Source URLs, file sizes and SHA-256 hashes |
| `pilot-xml-validation.json` | Original-filing validation of the Dodge & Cox and BlackRock positions |

The cached PDFs and principal-row images preserve the source material reviewed. The main ownership findings are this pilot's calculations, not figures published by HSBC or the SEC. No commercial ownership database was accessed, and no missing ownership was imputed.
